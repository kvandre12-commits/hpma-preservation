# Proposed preservation demonstration — scope v0.1

**Status:** concept only; not implemented or authorized.

## Purpose

Preserve representative interaction, atmosphere, interface, and historical context from *Harry Potter: Magic Awakened* through a bounded educational exhibit. The objective is not to replace, compete with, or impersonate the commercial service.

## Candidate visitor journey

1. Read a short release and preservation context panel.
2. Choose from pre-approved demonstration characters.
3. Enter one bounded Hogwarts environment.
4. Follow one movement and interaction tutorial.
5. Attend one short scripted class or equivalent mechanic.
6. Play one local scripted duel against a non-player opponent.
7. Review a gallery of source-linked development and community history.
8. Exit; no persistent public account is required.

Every element is independently removable. Rights holders can approve a smaller subset without approving the whole design.

## Explicit exclusions

- Official account authentication or migration.
- Purchases, virtual currency, advertisements, loot mechanics or payment SDKs.
- Public chat, direct messages, guild administration or user-generated names.
- Competitive matchmaking, public leaderboards or live-service events.
- Scraped player databases or private telemetry.
- Anti-cheat bypass, credential emulation or access to the official production service.
- Bulk downloadable commercial assets.
- Claims of official continuation, endorsement or franchise canon.

## Technical shape

Preferred order:

1. A rights-holder-provided museum build or offline mode.
2. An approved client connected to a rights-holder-provided demonstration backend.
3. A clean-room backend implementing only authorized observable behavior.
4. If none is authorized, a non-playable documentary exhibit using licensed captures and original explanatory material.

The public project will not begin options 2 or 3 without written authorization defining protocol observation, client use, assets, trademarks, hosting, security testing and distribution.

## Content manifest

Before implementation, create a signed schedule containing every allowed:

- executable and version;
- environment or level;
- character and cosmetic;
- animation and visual asset;
- text and localization;
- sound, music and voice performance;
- trademark and attribution;
- capture, screenshot and promotional image.

Anything absent from the schedule is excluded by default.

## Operations

Suggested initial limits:

- non-commercial;
- one named operator;
- private technical evaluation first;
- no more than [AGREED CONCURRENCY] visitors;
- no public API;
- no remote administration exposed to visitors;
- allowlisted content and actions;
- encrypted transport where networked;
- minimal logs with a fixed deletion period;
- no secrets embedded in the client;
- documented shutdown and takedown procedure;
- reproducible builds and cryptographic release receipts.

## Privacy

Use anonymous temporary sessions. Collect no birth dates, payment data, address books, advertising identifiers, official-service credentials, or third-party account tokens. If aggregate exhibit metrics are requested, define them in the agreement and publish the retention period.

## Preservation outputs

Subject to permission:

- an executable exhibit package;
- source for project-authored components;
- build instructions and dependency archive;
- content-rights manifest;
- operator manual and threat model;
- hashes and provenance receipts;
- accessibility and compatibility notes;
- deposit copy for an agreed institution.

## Success criteria

The exhibit succeeds when it is historically useful, technically reproducible, privacy-preserving, clearly unofficial, bounded by written permission, and still understandable after its original maintainers leave. Concurrent-user count is not a preservation metric. Neither is pretending a private server with a stolen asset dump is a museum.
